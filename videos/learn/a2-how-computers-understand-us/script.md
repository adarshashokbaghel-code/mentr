# Lesson A2 — How Computers Understand Us

**Unit:** CS Unit 1 · How Computers Work  
**Chapter:** 2 of 5 · Level Easy  
**Audience:** Class 3–5 (ages ~8–11) · narrated, easy words  
**Style:** Real class mentoring — warm Indian English, ask → wait → check, no jargon dump  
**Syllabus teach:**
1. Computers only understand on and off — like a light switch  
2. On = 1, off = 0; a group of switches can count and spell  
3. No long binary arithmetic — just the idea  

**Outcomes after video:** explain binary as on/off lights · read a short 0/1 pattern (up to 3 lights)  

---

## Full mentoring script (spoken)

### Opening — welcome + chapter card
1. Hello again, champ! Welcome back to Mentr Learn. Nice to see you.
2. Yesterday we learned what a computer is. Today we go one step deeper.
3. This is Chapter two. The lesson is: How Computers Understand Us.
4. Say it with me… How… Computers… Understand… Us.
5. Brilliant. Let’s open the secret of computers.

### Hook — mystery
6. Here is a puzzle. Computers are clever… but they do not speak Hindi or English like we do.
7. So how do they understand games, photos, and messages?
8. Pause. Guess for three seconds… how does a computer “talk” inside?
9. Keep your guess. We will prove it with light switches.

### Big idea — only on and off
10. The big idea is tiny and powerful. Inside a computer, everything is only two states.
11. Like a light switch — ON… or OFF. That is all.
12. Say with me: On… or off. Only two choices!
13. We write ON as the number one. We write OFF as the number zero.
14. One means on. Zero means off. Easy!

### Story — three switches (binary idea)
15. Now imagine three light switches in a row. Left, middle, right.
16. Each switch can be on or off. So each switch is a one or a zero.
17. Look: switch off, off, on — we write zero, zero, one.
18. Or on, off, on — we write one, zero, one.
19. That pattern of ones and zeros is called binary. Bi means two. Two choices only.
20. You do not need hard maths today. Just remember: binary is the computer’s light-switch language.

### Hands-on — read the lights
21. Your turn to read! I show a pattern. You say the zeros and ones out loud.
22. Pattern one: off, off, on. You say… zero, zero, one!
23. Pattern two: on, on, off. You say… one, one, zero!
24. Pattern three: on, off, on. You say… one, zero, one!
25. Super reading. That is how computers store tiny bits of information.

### Soft checkpoint (matches syllabus)
26. Practice check — same kind of question you will see later.
27. If one means on, what does one-zero-one mean on three lights?
28. Think… left on, middle off, right on.
29. Yes! One-zero-one means on, off, on. Three lights telling a tiny story.

### Why it matters
30. Why learn this? Because every photo, every game, every message becomes ones and zeros inside.
31. Your brain uses words. The computer uses on and off — then turns it back into pictures and sound for you.
32. So when someone says “binary,” you can smile and say: light-switch language!

### Recap + bridge to quiz
33. Lock it in. Computers understand on and off. On is one. Off is zero.
34. A row of ones and zeros is binary — the computer’s special language.
35. You can read a short pattern like one-zero-one as on, off, on.
36. Chapter two done. How Computers Understand Us — excellent work today!
37. Next, a short quiz to practise. Tap Finish when you are ready. See you there, champ!
