# Lesson A5 — Being Safe Online

**Unit:** CS Unit 1 · How Computers Work  
**Chapter:** 5 of 5 · Level Easy  
**Audience:** Class 3–5 (ages ~8–11) · brisk, energetic narration  
**Style:** Quick-fire mentoring — short lines, fast games, minimal pauses  
**Syllabus teach:**
1. Never share full name + school + home together with strangers  
2. Passwords are secrets — even from friends  
3. Tell a trusted adult if a chat feels odd  

**Outcomes:** list three things never to post publicly · know who to tell if something online feels unsafe  

---

## Full mentoring script (spoken)

### Opening
1. Hi champ! Welcome to Chapter five, the last chapter of Unit one!
2. You already know what a computer is, how it thinks, its devices, and how websites talk.
3. Today's big one: Being Safe Online!
4. You'll learn three super rules that protect you, just like a shield.

### Hook
5. Imagine you're playing a game online, and a message pops up: Hi! I'm ten too. Want to be friends?
6. Quick question. Can you see who is really typing? Think! *(wait)*
7. Nope! Online, anyone can pretend to be anyone. It could be a kid, or a grown-up stranger.
8. That's why smart champs follow three safety rules. Let's go!

### Rule 1 — Private info stays private
9. Rule one: private info stays private.
10. Private info means your full name, your school, your home address, and your phone number.
11. One piece looks small. But put them together, and it's like a map that leads a stranger straight to your door!
12. So what can you share? Your favourite colour, a game nickname, or your drawing. Totally fine!
13. Say it with me: private stays private!

### Game — Safe or unsafe?
14. Game time! Safe post, or unsafe post? Answer fast!
15. Post one: I love mango ice cream! *(wait)*
16. Safe! Everybody can know that.
17. Post two: Hi, I'm Riya from Green Park School, and I live on Rose Street! *(wait)*
18. Unsafe! Name, school and street, all together. That's the map again!
19. Post three: a photo of my new drawing. *(wait)*
20. Safe! Just don't show your school badge or house number in the picture.

### Rule 2 — Passwords are secret
21. Rule two: your password is a secret key.
22. It locks your games and accounts, just like a key locks your home.
23. Keep it secret from everyone, even your best friend! Only your mum and dad can know it.
24. A weak password is easy to guess, like one two three four, or your own name.
25. A strong one is long and mixed up, with words, numbers and symbols. Like Tiger, Jumps, seven, Mango!
26. Tip: make your own. Never copy the one on the screen!

### Game — Which is stronger?
27. Your turn! Which is stronger: a b c one two three, or Blue Kite Runs forty two? *(wait)*
28. Blue Kite Runs forty two! It's longer and mixed up, so it's way harder to guess.

### Rule 3 — Odd chat? Tell a trusted adult
29. Rule three: if a chat feels odd, tell a trusted adult.
30. Odd means someone asks for photos, asks where you live, or says: don't tell your parents. Those are red flags!
31. Then do three things. Stop. Don't reply. Tell a grown-up you trust.
32. Who? Your mum or dad, your teacher, or your nani and nana.
33. And remember, you are never in trouble for telling. Telling is brave!

### Practice check (syllabus)
34. Practice check, just like the quiz!
35. A new online friend asks for your home address. What do you do? A: tell them. B: tell only your street. Or C: don't share, and tell a trusted adult. *(wait)*
36. C! Never share your address. Tell a grown-up right away. Well done!

### Recap + quiz bridge
37. Let's lock it in! Rule one: private info stays private.
38. Rule two: passwords are secret, even from friends.
39. Rule three: odd chat? Stop, don't reply, and tell a trusted adult.
40. And three things never to post: your full name, your school, and your home address.
41. Chapter five done, and Unit one complete! You're a safe online champ!
42. Now a quick quiz. Tap Finish, and let's go!
