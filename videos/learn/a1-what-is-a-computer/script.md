# Lesson A1 — What Is a Computer?

**Unit:** CS Unit 1 · How Computers Work  
**Chapter:** 1 of 5 · Level Easy  
**Audience:** Class 3–5 (ages ~8–11) · narrated, easy words  
**Style:** Real class mentoring — warm, clear, ask → wait → check  
**Syllabus teach:**
1. Computer = input → process → output  
2. Phone, laptop, tablet = computers · toaster ≠ computer  
3. Story: sending a message vs switching on a bulb  

**Outcomes after video:** name IPO · tell computer from simple gadget  

---

## Full mentoring script (spoken)

### Opening — welcome + chapter card
1. Hello, champ! Welcome back to Mentr Learn. I am so happy you are here today.
2. Today we start Computer Science — Unit one — How Computers Work.
3. This is Chapter one. The name of our lesson is: What Is a Computer?
4. Say the title with me, nice and slow… What… Is… A… Computer?
5. Great! Keep that title in your mind. Ready? Let's go.

### Hook — curiosity
6. Look around your home. Phones, TVs, fans, toasters, lamps… so many machines!
7. But wait. Are all of them computers? Hmm. What do you think?
8. Pause and think for a moment. Point to one thing near you that you think is a computer.
9. Good thinking! We will check your answer together at the end.

### Big idea — three jobs
10. Here is the big secret. A computer is a special machine with three jobs.
11. Job one — INPUT. That means something goes in.
12. Job two — PROCESS. That means the machine thinks, or works on it.
13. Job three — OUTPUT. That means something comes out — an answer, a picture, or a sound.
14. Let's say the three jobs together: Input… Process… Output!
15. Wonderful. If a machine does all three jobs, we can call it a computer.

### Story A — sending a message (computer)
16. Let's try a real story. Imagine you send a message to your friend on a phone.
17. First, you type the words with your fingers. That is INPUT — words going in.
18. Next, the phone works on your message — it gets it ready to send. That is PROCESS.
19. Then your friend sees the message on their screen. That is OUTPUT.
20. So — input, process, output. Your phone did all three jobs. Your phone is a computer!
21. A laptop can do the same. A tablet can do the same. They are computers too.

### Story B — switching on a bulb (not a full computer)
22. Now, another story. You press a switch, and a bulb lights up.
23. The switch sends power. The bulb gets hot and shines. That is useful!
24. But the bulb is not taking many kinds of input and thinking about a message or a game.
25. It mainly turns light on or off. So a simple bulb — or a toaster that only heats bread — is not a computer.
26. Remember: computers take input, process it, and show output in a smarter way.

### Interactive check — computer or not?
27. Quick game time! I will name something. You say YES if it is a computer, or NO if it is not.
28. Ready? Laptop… Yes! A laptop is a computer.
29. Phone… Yes! A phone is a computer.
30. Tablet… Yes! A tablet is a computer.
31. Toaster… No! A toaster is not a computer — it only heats bread.
32. Lamp… No! A lamp is not a computer.
33. You are doing amazing. High five!

### Soft checkpoint (matches syllabus)
34. One more thinking question — the same kind you will see later in your check.
35. Which one is a computer: a laptop, a lamp, or a bicycle bell?
36. If you said laptop — you are right! Because it takes input, processes, and shows output.
37. A lamp and a bicycle bell are helpful, but they are not computers.

### Recap + bridge to quiz
38. Let's lock it in. A computer has three jobs: input, process, and output.
39. Phone, laptop, tablet — computers. Toaster, lamp — not computers.
40. You learned Chapter one: What Is a Computer? Brilliant work today!
41. Next, you will play a short quiz to practise. Tap Finish when you are ready. See you there, champ!
