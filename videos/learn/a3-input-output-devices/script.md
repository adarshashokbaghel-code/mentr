# Lesson A3 — Input & Output Devices

**Unit:** CS Unit 1 · How Computers Work  
**Chapter:** 3 of 5 · Level Easy  
**Audience:** Class 3–5 (ages ~8–11) · narrated, easy words  
**Style:** Real class mentoring — warm Indian English, ask → wait → check, one idea at a time  
**Syllabus teach:**
1. Input devices send information in: keyboard, mouse, mic, camera  
2. Output devices send information out: screen, speaker, printer  
3. Some devices do both (touchscreen)  

**Outcomes after video:** sort common devices into input or output · give one example of each from home or school  

---

## Full mentoring script (spoken)

### Opening — welcome + bridge + chapter card
1. Hello, champ! Welcome back to Mentr Learn. I am so happy you came back for class.
2. Quick memory check. In Chapter one, we learned the three jobs: input, process, output. In Chapter two, we learned binary — ones and zeros.
3. Today is Chapter three. The lesson is: Input and Output Devices.
4. Say it with me… Input… and… Output… Devices.
5. Wonderful! Today we will meet real machines you can touch and use.

### Hook — the closed box
6. Imagine a computer that is just a closed box. No keyboard, no mouse, no screen, no speaker.
7. Two questions. How would you tell it what to do? And how would it show you the answer?
8. Pause and think for three seconds…
9. Here is the answer. A computer needs doors — a door for things to come in, and a door for things to go out.

### Big idea — doors are devices
10. These doors are special machines called devices.
11. Input devices bring information IN to the computer.
12. Output devices send information OUT from the computer, to you.
13. Remember input, process, output from Chapter one? Input devices do the first job. Output devices do the last job.
14. Say with me: In… comes in. Out… goes out!

### Tour 1 — input devices
15. Let's meet the input devices. These are the ones that take things from you.
16. Number one: the keyboard. When you type letters and numbers, they go into the computer. Input!
17. Number two: the mouse. You move it, point, and click. Your click goes in. Input!
18. Number three: the microphone. It listens to your voice and sends it in. Input!
19. Number four: the camera. It takes your picture or video and sends it in. Input!
20. Keyboard, mouse, microphone, camera — all four bring information in.

### Tour 2 — output devices
21. Now, the output devices. These give things back to you.
22. The screen shows you words, pictures, and videos. Output!
23. The speaker plays sounds and music for your ears. Output!
24. The printer puts your work on paper, so you can hold it. Output!
25. Screen, speaker, printer — they all send information out to you.

### Tricky pair + the super trick
26. Now a tricky one. A microphone and a speaker both deal with sound. So which is which?
27. The microphone listens. It takes your sound in. So it is input.
28. The speaker talks. It gives sound out to you. So it is output.
29. Here is a super trick. Ask: does it take from me, or give to me? Takes from me — input. Gives to me — output.
30. Say the trick with me: Takes — input. Gives — output!

### Both jobs
31. Some devices are extra clever. Look at a touchscreen, like on a tablet or phone.
32. When you tap it with your finger, your touch goes in. That is input.
33. And the same screen shows you pictures and games. That is output.
34. So a touchscreen does both jobs — input and output!
35. A headset with a mic is also both. The mic takes your voice in, and the earphones give sound out.

### Sorting game (ask → wait → answer)
36. Game time! I show a device. You shout: input, output, or both!
37. Keyboard… input or output? *(wait)* → Input! You type, it goes in.
38. Screen… input or output? *(wait)* → Output! It shows pictures to you.
39. Microphone… input or output? *(wait)* → Input! It listens to your voice.
40. Printer… input or output? *(wait)* → Output! It gives you paper.
41. Camera… input or output? *(wait)* → Input! It takes your picture in.
42. Speaker… input or output? *(wait)* → Output! It gives sound to your ears.
43. Last one. Touchscreen… careful! *(wait)* → Both! Your tap goes in, and pictures come out.
44. Seven out of seven? Give yourself a big clap, champ!

### Real-life story — video call with Nani
45. Now a real-life story. You are on a video call with your Nani.
46. Your laptop camera sees your smile, and the microphone hears your hello. Those are input devices.
47. The computers do the process job and send it across the internet.
48. On Nani's side, the screen shows your face and the speaker plays your voice. Output devices!
49. And when Nani says, hello beta, the same thing happens back to you. Input, process, output!

### Device detective (home / school)
50. Your turn to be a detective. Look around your home or classroom.
51. Find one input device and one output device. Point to them. I will wait.
52. Here is an example. A TV remote — you press buttons, so it is input. The TV screen shows the cartoon, so it is output.
53. Great detective work, champ!

### Soft checkpoint (matches syllabus)
54. Practice check — the same kind of question you will see later.
55. Is a speaker an input device or an output device?
56. Use the trick. Does it take from you, or give to you?
57. Yes! A speaker is output, because it gives sound out to you.

### Recap + bridge to quiz
58. Let's lock it in. Input devices bring information in: keyboard, mouse, microphone, camera.
59. Output devices send information out: screen, speaker, printer.
60. Some devices, like a touchscreen, do both.
61. And the trick: takes from you — input. Gives to you — output.
62. Chapter three done. Input and Output Devices — fantastic work today!
63. Next, a short quiz to practise. Tap Finish when you are ready. See you there, champ!
