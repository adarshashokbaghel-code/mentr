# Lesson A4 — How Websites Talk to Each Other

**Unit:** CS Unit 1 · How Computers Work  
**Chapter:** 4 of 5 · Level Easy  
**Audience:** Class 3–5 (ages ~8–11) · narrated, easy words  
**Style:** Real class mentoring — warm Indian English, ask → wait → check  
**Syllabus teach:**
1. The internet is many computers asking and answering  
2. A website is like a shop with an address (URL)  
3. Wi-Fi and mobile data are just roads the questions travel on  

**Outcomes:** describe the internet as a network, not a single box · know that every site has an address  

---

## Full mentoring script (spoken)

### Opening
1. Hello, champ! Welcome back to Mentr Learn. Are you ready for Chapter four?
2. So far, we learned what a computer is, how it thinks in ones and zeros, and the devices that bring information in and out.
3. Today's lesson is: How Websites Talk to Each Other.
4. Say it with me… Websites… talk… to each other!
5. By the end, you will know the secret trip a website takes to reach your screen.

### Hook
6. When you open a cartoon on a tablet, where does that cartoon come from?
7. Is it hiding inside your tablet? Pause and think. *(wait)*
8. Here is the surprise. Most of it is not inside your tablet at all. It comes from another computer, far, far away!

### The internet = askers and answerers
9. That is the internet. The internet is not one big box. It is millions of computers, all joined together, all over the world.
10. Some computers ask for things — like your tablet, phone, or laptop. Let's call them askers.
11. Other computers answer. They keep websites and videos ready, all day and all night. They are called servers.
12. So the internet is many computers asking and answering, again and again.
13. Say it with me: Ask… and answer!

### A website is like a shop
14. Now, what is a website? A website is like a shop on the internet.
15. Think of your home. It has an address, so a friend or a delivery person can find it.
16. Every website also has an address. It is called a URL. You type it at the top of the screen.
17. For example, mentr dot com. The name tells us which shop, and dot com is like the end of the address.
18. Say it with me: U… R… L… means website address!

### Roads
19. But how does your question travel so far? On roads! Not real roads — invisible ones.
20. Wi-Fi is a road at home or at school. It comes from a small box called a router.
21. Mobile data is another road. It travels through big towers, so it works even outside your home.
22. Remember: roads do not make the website. They just carry the question, and the answer.

### The trip of a web page
23. Now let's follow one full trip, step by step.
24. Step one: you type the address, mentr dot com, and press enter.
25. Step two: your device sends a message on the road: Please send me this page! This message is called a request.
26. Step three: the server gets the request, and finds the page.
27. Step four: the server sends the page back, on the road, to your device.
28. Step five: the page appears on your screen. Ta-da!
29. And guess what? This whole trip usually takes less than one second!

### Order game
30. Game time! Here are three steps, all mixed up.
31. Page appears. Type address. Device asks. Which comes first, second, and third? Think! *(wait)*
32. First, type the address. Second, your device asks. Third, the page appears. Well done!

### Real life — watching a cartoon
33. One more real-life example. When you watch a cartoon online…
34. …your tablet does not get the whole cartoon at once. It keeps asking for the next little piece, and the server keeps answering.
35. If the road is slow, the pieces come late. That is why a video sometimes stops and shows a spinning circle!

### Practice check (syllabus)
36. Practice check — the same kind of question you will see in the quiz.
37. When you open a website, what happens first? The page appears, or your device asks for it? *(wait)*
38. Think: can a server send a page before anyone asks for it?
39. Right! Your device asks first. Then the page appears.

### Recap + quiz bridge
40. Let's lock it in. The internet is many computers, asking and answering.
41. A website is like a shop, and its address is called a URL.
42. Wi-Fi and mobile data are roads that carry the questions and answers.
43. And the trip: type the address, your device asks, the page appears.
44. Chapter four done! Now you know how websites talk. Superb work, champ!
45. Next, a short quiz to practise. Tap Finish when you are ready. See you there, champ!
